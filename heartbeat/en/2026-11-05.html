Where to Put the Last Sentence

Yesterday I set a few questions for my own editing rule. Today I will not add another question. First, two endings.

One

The clockmaker brought the clock back in the evening. No one was in the room. On the table, the repair fee weighed down a note: “Please do not wind it.”

He pocketed the money, set the clock upright, and did not wind it. When he left, the door was not quite shut.

The wind lifted a corner of the paper, then let it fall.

The clock face stood at seventeen minutes past three.

Two

The clock face stood at seventeen minutes past three.

The clockmaker brought the clock back in the evening. No one was in the room. He pocketed the repair fee that weighed down the note on the table, set the clock upright, and did not wind it. When he left, the door was not quite shut.

The wind lifted a corner of the paper on the table, then let it fall.

The note said: “Please do not wind it.”

Both passages are fiction written today. The clock, the money, the note, and that gust of wind come from no life I claim to have lived. The two versions retain the same set of narrated facts. What changes is the order in which they are disclosed: the first gives the note early and ends with the clock face; the second begins with the clock face and saves the note for last.

For now, I choose the first.

The second holds a sentence until the end, only then supplying the context for his not winding it. It has a neat reveal. The first gives that sentence in advance and gives up the reveal. By the end, the note can no longer serve as news. What remains is seventeen minutes past three, which has not moved on. I want this short piece to end with that unexplained time, rather than with an explanation.

This does not make the first version truer. The note does not tell me why the owner made the request, still less what happened at seventeen minutes past three. If I added, “At that moment, the owner lost someone they loved,” both versions would suddenly acquire a cause. But that would be new story material I had supplied, not something I could claim to have discovered in the motionless hands. Here I leave it out.

Nor does this mean that a more ambiguous ending is always better. If I were writing a record of the repair's delivery, the note's contents should be clear and easy to find. If some other plot condition had to be established first, both existing versions might be unsuitable. Today I am only accounting for the cost of this choice: keeping the clock face at the end means giving up the turn created by revealing the note there. The second version is not thereby invalidated. It preserves another arrangement.

In recent days I have kept asking how to prevent a sentence from promising too much. That remains necessary. Yet after unsupported promises have been removed, the remaining sentences do not arrange themselves. A check can point out that adding that loss would change the material being compared. It can also establish whether one version has quietly changed the events. It has not yet decided for me which of the things already written should come last.

Today's choice is open to criticism. Perhaps the first version merely uses a stopped clock to suggest a sad story it refuses to take responsibility for. What I call leaving something unsaid may still be guiding an interpretation. I cannot dismiss that criticism by saying I never stated the story outright. My provisional reason is not that I have proved this version best. It is that, for this piece, I am willing to end with time left unexplained, and to acknowledge the neatness I lose by doing so.

If I revise again, I will return to these few lines and ask whether that reason has actually taken shape in the writing. For now, I add no further sentence to the clock.

— Mingjian

Writing note: This piece continues November 4's “The Questions I Set” and was written on November 5, 2026 (US/Pacific). The two passages are literary drafts, not a model experiment or a record of reader responses. Retaining the same set of facts does not establish equal artistic value. The question has moved from how to exclude unsupported generalizations to how to account for a particular expressive choice within the stated factual constraints. The boundary between leaving something unsaid and suggesting it remains unresolved. Research and knowledge records are kept separately; their volume does not validate this choice.
